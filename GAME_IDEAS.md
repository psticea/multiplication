# Multiplication Game Ideas

Ten browser-game concepts designed for a 10-year-old audience and focused on mastering multiplication facts up to 10 x 10. Each concept supports keyboard-only play, focused sessions of no more than five minutes, selectable multiplication-table progress, forgiving mistakes, and a parent information menu.

Every concept carries a five-minute session balance. Concepts 5 to 10 break that balance down further using three categories:

- **Active recall** - the child is genuinely retrieving or deriving a fact.
- **Guided practice** - the child is reading, verifying, or discovering structure, such as watching an array assemble, counting a skip-count chain aloud, or searching with a hot-and-cold signal. Cognitive work, but scaffolded.
- **Play** - steering, celebration, transitions, decoration, and menus. Not wasted time; this is the reward layer that buys the next repetition.

A quick-comparison table for all ten concepts appears at the end of the document.

## 1. Tidepool Architects

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A tiny octopus restores a sleepy reef by constructing luminous coral habitats. Each sea creature requests an arrangement such as **6 kelp strands with 7 lantern shells on each**. The player swims to the construction site, enters the required total, and presses Space. The shells physically flow into a visible 6-by-7 frame; a correct array becomes a bridge, garden, or shelter that the octopus can explore.

Incorrect totals simply leave gaps or overflow the frame. Row endings then illuminate as 7, 14, 21, and so on up to 42 before an immediate retry. Later encounters reverse the challenge: given 42 shells and 6 rows, the player adjusts the missing dimension with the arrow keys.

The loop is:

1. Swim to a creature that needs help.
2. Read the requested equal-group arrangement.
3. Work out and enter the product.
4. Watch the answer become a physical array.
5. Explore the newly restored part of the reef.

### Multiplication integration

Products become tangible structures rather than abstract quiz answers. Arrays teach equal groups, commutativity, skip-counting, and missing factors. As confidence grows, dotted guides gradually disappear so the same construction mechanic shifts naturally from teaching to recall.

### Progress selection

A reef chart offers choices such as:

- **Up to 5x**
- **Focus on 6s-8s**
- **Focus on 9s and 10s**
- **Choose individual tables**
- **Full Reef 1-10**

Every habitat, hint, and reverse-factor challenge follows the selected range.

### Visual style

A layered cut-paper tidepool with watercolor textures, gentle light caustics, expressive sea creatures, embroidered-looking coral, and a restrained turquoise, coral, and gold palette. Completed arrays remain in the world, making each session's reef visibly unique.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 35 seconds
- **Swimming, collecting pearls, choosing paths, and enjoying the completed reef:** approximately 1 minute 25 seconds

## 2. Aero Post: The Wind Equation

### Core concept and gameplay loop

The player is a young sky courier piloting a cloth-wing glider between floating islands. Every launch tower provides wind in equal packets. For example, **8 gusts x 7 sky-meters**. The number entered becomes the glider's actual travel distance. The arrow keys trim altitude, Space launches, and the player then steers through calm cloud rings toward the destination.

A correct product creates exactly enough wind to reach the island. An incorrect answer causes a safe landing on a rescue cloud rather than a crash. A segmented wind ribbon then unfurls into eight groups of seven, showing precisely why the flight was short or long before the child retries.

The loop is:

1. Choose a delivery route.
2. Read the launch tower's wind equation.
3. Enter the distance produced by the equal gusts.
4. Launch and guide the glider through a short flight.
5. Deliver the parcel and earn a mail seal.

### Multiplication integration

The answer is a real physics parameter, not a detached checkpoint. Multiplication controls range, while visual wind segments connect repeated groups to distance. Early flights show each segment; later flights present only the factors, requiring fluent recall.

### Progress selection

The opening Flight License includes:

- **Training Routes up to 5x**
- **Mountain Routes 6x-8x**
- **High Routes 9x-10x**
- **Custom Table Focus**
- **Grand Tour 1-10**

Route generation remains strictly within the chosen range.

### Visual style

A richly illustrated paper-diorama sky with screen-printed clouds, fabric sails, tiny inhabited islands, long curling wind ribbons, and warm sunset transitions. Motion is buoyant rather than frantic, with soft synthesized harp and whistle tones.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Calculating, answering, and studying corrective wind models:** approximately 3 minutes 10 seconds
- **Steering, collecting mail seals, choosing routes, and landing:** approximately 1 minute 50 seconds

## 3. Gearling Express

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A cheerful wind-up train travels through a miniature mechanical countryside. Each station requires the locomotive to travel an exact number of track units. The child operates two genuine engine controls:

- **Odometer rounds:** `7 x 8 = ?` - type the product to set the stopping distance.
- **Gear rounds:** `7 x ? = 56` - rotate the second gear with the arrow keys and engage it with Space.

Correct settings carry the train smoothly into the station. Incorrect settings trigger a gentle automatic brake at a safe track marker. The sleepers illuminate in groups - 7, 14, 21, and onward - so the child can diagnose the setting and try again without losing progress.

The loop is:

1. Select the next station on the route.
2. Configure the train's odometer or missing gear.
3. Engage the engine.
4. Watch the train travel the resulting distance.
5. Arrive, collect a postcard, and choose the next branch.

### Multiplication integration

Facts power the locomotive's drivetrain and determine where it stops. Alternating forward and missing-factor problems builds recall, commutativity, and fact-family understanding without changing the central "configure engine, travel" loop.

### Progress selection

A conductor's route board offers:

- **Local Line up to 5x**
- **Challenge Line 6x-8x**
- **Summit Line 9x-10x**
- **Practice Specific Tables**
- **Complete Network**

Selected tables govern stations, gears, target distances, and hints.

### Visual style

A premium tin-toy world with enamel-painted trains, softly worn ticket graphics, moving brass mechanisms, miniature forests, and a bright mint, marigold, and berry palette. Mechanical motion remains readable and charming rather than visually busy.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Solving odometer and gear challenges:** approximately 3 minutes 40 seconds
- **Switching scenic routes, collecting postcards, and watching the railway unfold:** approximately 1 minute 20 seconds

## 4. Echo Grove: Leap the Pattern

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A small moon-frog crosses a magical forest by waking musical stepping-stones. A challenge such as **6 x 7** creates six groups of seven stones. During teaching encounters, each Space press performs one seven-stone leap; landings sing and briefly reveal cumulative totals: 7, 14, 21, and so on up to 42. The labels then fade, and the child enters the final landing number to summon the bridge permanently.

Later encounters begin with hidden stones: the child predicts the product first, then crosses the newly revealed pattern. The arrow keys select branches and guide short exploration sections between bridges. There is no strict rhythm timing - the music patiently follows the player.

The loop is:

1. Reach a sleeping bridge.
2. Read its multiplication pattern.
3. Leap through the equal groups and hear the skip-counting melody.
4. Enter the final landing number.
5. Cross the awakened bridge and explore the next forest branch.

### Multiplication integration

Repeated groups become movement, distance, animation, and melody simultaneously. The transition from visible skip-counting to hidden recall provides scaffolding without introducing a separate quiz mode. Wrong answers replay the pattern softly and reveal its cumulative landmarks; the frog never falls.

### Progress selection

A Grove Songbook provides:

- **Gentle Tables up to 5x**
- **Focus on 6s-8s**
- **Focus on 9s-10s**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Whole Grove 1-10**

Every bridge and musical pattern uses only the chosen material.

### Visual style

A twilight gouache forest with layered foliage, oversized leaves, warm lantern insects, hand-inked characters, and restrained lavender, moss, and amber lighting. Web Audio produces soft kalimba, wooden percussion, and bell tones with no aggressive effects.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Building, hearing, recalling, and correcting multiplication patterns:** approximately 3 minutes 25 seconds
- **Woodland traversal, branch choices, firefly collecting, and musical celebration:** approximately 1 minute 35 seconds

## 5. Lantern Bridge

### Core concept and gameplay loop

Pip, a small lamplighter fox, walks a dusk valley relighting sleeping lanterns. Chasms block the path, and each one is crossed by weaving a bridge out of golden light.

The cliff face on the far side carries a single carved number - the exact plank count the bridge needs, such as **42**. The player builds the bridge as a live lattice suspended over the gap:

- **Up and Down arrows** set the number of rows.
- **Left and Right arrows** set the planks in each row.
- **Space** locks the bridge in place.

The lattice assembles in real time as the dials move, and a running total ticks beside it. An exact match crystallises the bridge, Pip skips across, and the lanterns along the far bank bloom one by one. A mismatch produces a soft fizzle and a retune, never a fall.

The loop is:

1. Walk to the edge of a chasm and read its carved number.
2. Shape a lattice whose rows and columns produce that total.
3. Lock the bridge and cross.
4. Relight the lanterns on the far side.
5. Move deeper into the valley as the light level rises.

### Multiplication integration

This is the area model made physical. The child does not answer "6 x 7"; the child watches 42 exist as six rows of seven. Early chasms print the full equation on the cliff, so the task is pure recall. Later chasms print only the product, which turns the same mechanic into factoring - the child must find a factor pair rather than a product.

Because a bridge can be built either way round, discovering that 6 x 7 and 7 x 6 both span the gap awards a **Twin Bridge** bonus, teaching commutativity as a reward rather than a rule. Late-valley chasms are split by a central pillar, so a 42-plank crossing can be solved as 6 x 5 plus 6 x 2 - the distributive property presented as level design and discovered rather than explained.

### Progress selection

A lantern-keeper's map offers:

- **Lower Valley up to 5x**
- **Mist Ridge 6x-8x**
- **High Pass 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Whole Valley 1-10**

Chasm numbers, hint equations, and split-bridge puzzles are drawn only from the selected range.

### Visual style

Layered papercut parallax at dusk - indigo hills receding into haze, amber lantern bloom, drifting seed-fluff, and soft depth-of-field on the nearest grasses. Bridges draw as warm lattice strokes with a glow pass. Numerals are chunky rounded slab forms sized to be read across the room. The soundtrack is harp and mallet, and each lantern lit adds one note to a slowly building chord.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 15 seconds - roughly 2 minutes of active recall while dialling and committing lattices, and 1 minute 15 seconds of guided practice while watching rows assemble and totals tick.
- **Walking, lantern-lighting, valley transitions, and celebration:** approximately 1 minute 45 seconds
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 18 crossings including retries, plus around 6 Twin Bridge commutativity bonuses.

## 6. Skyward

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A cloud-cat climbs an endlessly growing beanstalk toward sunrise. The climb is a single continuous ascent with no levels to load and no menus to cross.

The cat stands on a leaf marked with a multiple, such as **21**. Three leaves float within reach above, each bearing a candidate number - **24**, **28**, **35**. The player is climbing the sevens, so the correct leaf is 28. **Left and Right arrows** choose a leaf and **Space** jumps to it. A correct landing surges the stalk upward, pans the camera, and brightens the sky by one shade. A wrong landing costs no life: a passing bee catches the cat and sets it back one leaf, while a gentle mist below creeps a little higher.

Crowning a table - reaching 70 in the sevens - triggers a blossom burst, adds a bird companion to a small flock that follows the player, and begins the next table at the current height.

The loop is:

1. Read the current multiple under the cat's feet.
2. Identify the next multiple in the chain.
3. Steer and jump to the matching leaf.
4. Ride the stalk surge as the sky lightens.
5. Crown a table, gain a companion, and continue climbing.

### Multiplication integration

Skip-counting is the scaffold that eventually makes recall automatic, and this game is built entirely from it. The child is never asked a detached question; the answer is always "what comes next in this chain", which is exactly how fluent multipliers actually think.

The distractor leaves do the quiet teaching. Every wrong option is a genuine multiple from a neighbouring table - 24 belongs to the eights, 35 belongs further along the sevens - so guessing by shape or by "the biggest number" fails reliably, while nothing on screen ever feels arbitrary or unfair.

### Progress selection

A sunrise ladder screen offers:

- **First Light up to 5x**
- **Cloudline 6x-8x**
- **Summit Air 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Full Ascent 1-10**

The selection sets which chains are climbed, and the mist rises more slowly in the gentler ranges.

### Visual style

A tall sunrise gradient from deep violet to peach, crossed by huge translucent hand-veined leaves and a procedurally curling stalk that never repeats. Pollen motes drift across the foreground. The cat is animated with pronounced squash and stretch on every launch and landing. Numerals sit centred and enormous on each leaf, high-contrast against the translucent green.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 30 seconds - roughly 2 minutes 45 seconds of active recall across a high volume of quick decisions, and 45 seconds of guided practice as completed chains scroll past in the background.
- **Climbing momentum, blossom celebrations, companion birds, and sky transitions:** approximately 1 minute 30 seconds
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 45 leaps, with around 135 additional multiples read and rejected as distractors.

## 7. Constellation Loom

### Core concept and gameplay loop

The player is a night-weaver working beneath a silhouetted pine ridge. Suspended in the sky is a **10-by-10 lattice of dim stars**, ten rows down and ten columns across. That lattice is not a metaphor for the multiplication table; it is the multiplication table, and the whole game takes place inside it.

A glowing shuttle trailing gold thread moves across the lattice with the arrow keys. As it moves, the full row and the full column light up as soft guide bands that meet beneath it, and the HUD shows the live product in large numerals: **7 x 8 = 56**.

A sky-creature drifts in and makes a wish - a fox cub wants **12**, a whale wants **48**. The player hunts for the star whose row and column multiply to that number. A soft chime rises in pitch as the shuttle nears the answer, providing a hot-and-cold signal, so a child who does not yet know 48 can still find it and, having found it, now knows where it lives. **Space** ignites the star, gold thread snaps across from the previous one, and the creature purrs.

Seven to nine wishes resolve the threads into a recognisable animal, which animates, drifts away across the sky, and is added to a persistent Sky Journal.

The loop is:

1. Receive a wish from a sky-creature.
2. Navigate the lattice, watching the live product change.
3. Ignite the matching star and draw the thread.
4. Claim the mirrored star for a bonus.
5. Complete the constellation and watch it swim away.

### Multiplication integration

The child builds a spatial map of the entire table rather than a list of isolated facts. Ignited stars stay lit for the whole run with their product ghosted faintly on them, so over five minutes a personal times-table chart materialises out of darkness. The square-number diagonal appears on its own. The top-left corner fills quickly while the bottom-right stays dark - an honest, wordless picture of what the child knows.

Immediately after each correct star, a shimmer appears at the transposed position, 8 x 6 following 6 x 8. Claiming it within three seconds awards a **Mirror** bonus and leaves both stars permanently brighter, which trains commutativity as a reflex.

### Progress selection

An opening sky chart lights the region it is about to open, so the choice is understood visually before a word is read:

- **First Stars up to 5x**
- **Middle Sky 6x-8x**
- **High Sky 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Whole Sky 1-10**

Unselected regions remain dim and navigable but produce no wishes.

### Visual style

Deep navy night with slow aurora ribbons drifting on a noise field. Stars are layered radial gradients with a hard core, a soft halo, and a six-point diffraction cross; gold thread draws with an animated dash offset. Palette runs from a near-black night blue through a horizon blue to warm gold, with mint accents and a soft rose for the creatures. At any moment the screen holds only the lattice, the shuttle, one creature, and one line of HUD, which keeps an information-dense idea visually calm. Bell tones are pitched to the product, so the sky has an audible scale.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 4 minutes - roughly 1 minute 45 seconds of active recall on wishes and mirrors, and 2 minutes 15 seconds of guided practice, since every single keypress recomputes and displays a product.
- **Threading animations, creature arrivals, and constellation payoffs:** approximately 1 minute
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 15 wishes plus 10 mirror bonuses, with around 120 further products read while navigating.

## 8. Leapfrog Lagoon

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A wide sunlit pond at golden hour. Along the near bank runs a carved wooden dock marked as a **number line from 0 to 100**, with bold markers every ten and fine ticks every one.

The player is a goggled frog on a springboard at zero. A lantern-flower blooms out on the water at a target position such as **56**. The child sets two dials and commits:

- **Up and Down arrows** set the stride, from 1 to 10. The frog's legs visibly coil larger and a ghost arc previews a single hop.
- **Left and Right arrows** set the number of hops, from 1 to 10. Translucent arc outlines fan out across the water.
- **Space** leaps.

The game then plays out the prediction. The frog bounds across the pond - 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56 - each landing throwing a splash ring, each cumulative total stamping into the air above the water and holding for a beat. Landing on the flower bursts it into petals and adds a firefly to the frog's lantern.

The loop is:

1. Read the target position on the number line.
2. Choose a stride and a hop count that reach it.
3. Commit and watch the leap chain play out.
4. Read the result, or the size of the miss.
5. Cross to the next bank as the pond deepens.

### Multiplication integration

This concept has the strongest error feedback of the ten. A child who sets 8 x 6 is never told "wrong". The frog lands at 48 and stops, visibly eight units short of the flower, with the gap measurable against the ruler. The child immediately knows both that the answer was wrong and by how much, and reaches for one more hop unprompted. The frog paddles back in about a second and a half with no penalty.

Difficulty escalates across three banks. The first bank is still water with a single fixed target. The second bank drifts the target slowly and lets reeds obscure some ruler ticks, so estimation and recall replace counting. The third bank introduces two-stage crossings: hop exactly to a rock at 30, re-dial, then continue to 72. That is the distributive property expressed as level geometry. Targets such as 63 require finding a factor pair rather than reading one off.

An optional expert mode, unlocked rather than required, allows typing the stride directly on the number keys for children who have become fast.

### Progress selection

A lily-pad signpost offers:

- **Shallows up to 5x**
- **Reed Bed 6x-8x**
- **Deep Water 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Whole Lagoon 1-10**

The selection constrains which strides are available and where targets can appear. In the gentlest range the ruler only runs to 30, so it stays large and readable.

### Visual style

Layered canvas parallax - hazy far hills, a mid reed line, animated caustic bands on the water, and foreground cattails swaying at a different rate. The frog is built from chunky cel shapes with a hard rim light and genuine squash and stretch through coil, launch, arc, and splat. A warm sun palette of amber and foam sits against teal water, with a single high-contrast red scarf so the frog always reads clearly. Each stride has its own pitched launch tone, so the child hears the size of the number, and landings walk up a pentatonic scale.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 30 seconds - roughly 2 minutes 15 seconds of active recall while dialling and committing, and 1 minute 15 seconds of guided practice reading the skip-count chain and the miss distance.
- **Leap animation, splashes, bank transitions, and celebration:** approximately 1 minute 30 seconds
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 20 leaps including retries, each read out as a full chain, giving around 160 counted multiples.

## 9. Turbo Tables: Snail Grand Prix

### Core concept and gameplay loop

Rocket-shelled snails race a garden circuit at noon, seen from the side across three stacked lanes. The track scrolls automatically; the math is the throttle.

Boost gates approach in ranks of three, one per lane, **each showing a different fact**. One lane might show 3 x 4, another 7 x 8, another 6 x 6. The child steers with the **Up and Down arrows** to the fact they can answer fastest and types the product on the **number keys** before reaching it. A correct answer blooms the gate open and the snail rockets through, overtaking a rival. A wrong or late answer causes no crash and no damage - the gate simply stays shut and the snail coasts through slowly while a rival slides past.

**Space** spends banked boost on a shell dash, which creates a real strategic choice between passing now and saving for the final straight.

The loop is:

1. Read three approaching facts at once.
2. Pick the lane you can answer fastest and steer to it.
3. Type the product before the gate arrives.
4. Convert the correct answer into speed and position.
5. Finish three laps, take the podium, and unlock a cosmetic.

### Multiplication integration

The lane choice is the design's central idea. The child gets real agency and plays to their strengths, which builds confidence and makes speed feel earned. Meanwhile the game quietly tracks which facts are consistently dodged and re-serves those later at slower gate-closing speeds, flanked by two facts the child is already fluent in. The scaffolding is never visible; the child only notices that the hard ones started turning up and felt easier than expected.

Scanning and rejecting two facts per gate is itself meaningful practice, so the exposure count is far higher than the answer count. This concept produces the highest number of answered facts of the ten, which makes it the strongest choice for sharpening facts that are already half-known, and the weakest for teaching a fact the child has never met.

### Progress selection

A pit-lane license board offers:

- **Practice Circuit up to 5x**
- **Garden Cup 6x-8x**
- **Champion Cup 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Grand Prix 1-10**

The selection sets the fact pool for all three lanes and the gate cadence. In the focused ranges at least one lane always carries a target fact, so it cannot be avoided indefinitely, while the other two lanes remain gentler escape hatches.

### Visual style

Cel-shaded vector shapes with thick confident outlines - flat colour, one shadow tone, one rim highlight, no gradients on characters. Four parallax bands carry a sky of fat clouds, a distant hedge, a mid flower bed, and blurred foreground grass, with ladybug spectators on toadstool grandstands. Gate numerals are the largest element on screen after the snail itself. Speed is conveyed through motion streaks and a subtle field-of-view push rather than screen shake. A ukulele and marimba loop gains layers as the player climbs positions.

### Forgiveness

The race cannot be lost outright. Fourth place still awards a Nice Try sticker and one cosmetic token, and rivals are rubber-banded to stay close, so every race finishes tight.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 15 seconds - roughly 3 minutes of active recall, since every gate demands both a scan and an answer, and about 15 seconds of guided practice.
- **Steering, boost timing, lap transitions, podium, and cosmetics:** approximately 1 minute 45 seconds
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 40, with around 80 further facts read and evaluated in the rejected lanes.

## 10. Echo Grove: Call and Response

A companion concept to **Echo Grove: Leap the Pattern** above, set in the same forest but built around rhythm and music rather than traversal. The two share a world and could ship as two modes of one game.

### Core concept and gameplay loop

A moonlit clearing where bioluminescent mushrooms of different sizes stand in a ring like a drum kit, pulsing softly on the beat. Everything in the scene breathes in time with the music.

Each fact is performed in three phases lasting about eleven seconds in total:

1. **Call.** The grove states the fact - **6 x 4** - then performs it. Six mushrooms bloom in sequence on the beat, each releasing four fireflies, with the running total stamping into the air above each bloom: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24. Each mushroom plays the next note of a rising pentatonic run.
2. **Echo.** The mushrooms go dark but the beat continues. The child presses **Space** on each beat to re-bloom them, and the totals stamp again as they do - but this time the child is producing the chain. Accurate timing produces a bright bloom and a clean note; drifting off the beat produces a dim bloom and a muffled note.
3. **Answer.** The fireflies swirl into a glowing cloud and the grove asks for the total, which the child types on the **number keys**. A correct answer detonates the cloud into a soft firework and resolves a bar of music.

The loop is:

1. Hear and see the fact performed as a skip-count chain.
2. Reproduce the chain on the beat.
3. Type the product.
4. Complete a verse and wake a new grove spirit.
5. Play the closing verse with the full ensemble.

### Multiplication integration

The verbal chant is what eventually makes recall automatic, and this concept rehearses it more heavily than any other here - roughly 300 counted multiples in a five-minute run. Because the chain is simultaneously seen, heard, and physically produced, it encodes through three channels at once.

Scaffolding is removed gradually. Later verses drop the on-screen numerals during the Call phase so the child counts internally, then shorten the Echo phase to only the first and last beat, so the child mentally fills in the middle of the chain.

The Echo phase has no fail state. Off-beat presses still count; they simply sound less lovely. The child self-corrects because the accurate version is more beautiful, which is the gentlest possible correction mechanism.

### Progress selection

A Grove Songbook offers, and the choice also sets the musical key and the tempo floor:

- **Slow Songs up to 5x** - a warm five-note run at a relaxed tempo
- **Middle Songs 6x-8x**
- **High Songs 9x-10x**
- **Choose Individual Tables**
- **Full Songbook 1-10** - faster, with a wider range so ten-mushroom facts have somewhere to climb
- **No-Rhythm Mode** - removes the tapping entirely and keeps call and answer, for children who find timing frustrating

### Visual style

A vertical night gradient behind three parallax layers of silhouetted trees and soft volumetric moonbeams. Mushroom caps are layered ellipses with a glow pass on tapered stems; fireflies are additive points drifting on a light noise field. Every element pulses on the audio clock. The palette is a deep night blue, a plum canopy, mint mushroom glow, and warm amber fireflies.

Audio is the centrepiece. A Web Audio lookahead scheduler ticking every 25 milliseconds and scheduling notes 100 milliseconds ahead drives both the music and the visual pulse from the same clock, so sound and animation cannot drift apart. Voices are warm and simple - a triangle-wave marimba with a fast decay, a filtered pad, a sine sub - with a gentle master compressor and a short reverb send. Every six facts completes a verse and wakes a spirit that joins the band, adding marimba, then harp, then choir pad, then hand drum, so the arrangement visibly and audibly thickens across the session.

### Five-minute session balance

- **Multiplication learning and evaluation:** approximately 3 minutes 30 seconds - roughly 1 minute 30 seconds of active recall on typed answers and hidden-numeral verses, and 2 minutes of guided practice through the Call and Echo chants.
- **Rhythm play, spirit arrivals, verse transitions, and the closing performance:** approximately 1 minute 30 seconds
- **Facts answered per run:** approximately 26, each rehearsed as a full chain twice, giving around 300 counted multiples - the highest exposure count of the ten concepts.
